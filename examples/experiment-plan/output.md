# Experiment plan

**Hypothesis (from the card):** a repair-status chatbot will cut repair-status phone calls by at least 30% within 12 weeks of launch
**Owner (decides):** the owner of Harbourside Lettings
**Date:** before April

## Riskiest assumption
Tenants will choose an online status check over phoning the office. If they will not, the chatbot is paid for and unused, and the rest of the hypothesis does not matter. Whether the chatbot answers well is a second question, and only worth asking if this one passes.

## Method
A no-build version for four weeks, with nothing bought from the supplier:
1. Add a line to the existing repair confirmation email: "Check where your repair is up to: [link]". The link opens a plain web page showing that tenant's job status, read from the repairs log. A person at the office builds it by hand: a one-page status view from the log, using a private link per job, and no chatbot.
2. Count the clicks on the link, using the link's own click count.
3. In the same four weeks, tally repair-status calls on a sheet beside the phone, so the baseline stops being "not measured".

This tests whether tenants use an online status check, with a plain page where the chatbot would go. It does not test chat itself.

Why this is the cheapest honest test: it costs staff time only, it can be switched off, and it could show the belief is wrong if the click count is low and calls do not fall.

## Sample
All tenants who receive a repair confirmation email in the four weeks. The count is unknown until the test runs, because it depends on how many repairs are logged. With about 400 tenancies, expect low tens to a few hundred emails; that is an estimate, not a measurement. This is too few to claim a statistical result. It shows direction only: whether use is close to zero, modest or high.

## Schedule and cost
| Start | End | Staff hours | Money | Needs owner's yes? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First Monday after approval | Four weeks later | About 6 hours to build the page; 5 minutes a day to tally calls; 1 hour to read the result | 0 pounds, unless the page needs a paid link tool, in which case ask first | Yes: the change to the tenant email and the private link per job (it shows tenant repair information, so the owner should confirm the data protection officer is content) |

## Criteria (written before the test)
- Success: at least 1 in 5 tenants who got the link click it within 3 days, and the weekly repair-status call tally in weeks 3 and 4 is lower than in weeks 1 and 2. Both must hold. Then move to a chatbot trial, and sign nothing until the owner decides.
- Stop early if: any tenant is shown another tenant's job (switch the page off at once and tell the owner), or the guard measure (late or duplicate repair reports) rises above the tally for the first two weeks by more than a few cases.
- If the result is unclear (clicks between 1 in 10 and 1 in 5, or calls flat): run two more weeks with the link moved to the top of the email, then decide. Do not buy during that time.

## Read-back
| Number | Read from | Recorded by | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emails sent with the link | Repairs system send log | Office manager | End of each week |
| Clicks on the link | Link click count | Office manager | End of each week |
| Repair-status calls | Tally sheet at the phone | Whoever answers | Daily, totalled weekly |
| Late or duplicate repair reports | Repairs log | Maintenance supervisor | End of each week |

Report the result as it is, including a clear failure.

## Who could be affected, and what they are told
Tenants who get the email see one extra line and a link to their own job. No other change. Staff are told that the tally sheet is a test, not a measure of anyone's work.

## Decision
The owner decides at the end of week four, on the day the totals are in.
