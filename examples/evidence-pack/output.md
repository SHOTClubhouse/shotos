# Evidence pack

**Work:** Repair-reply drafting pilot, Harbourside Lettings
**Prepared by / date:** the agent, 3 November (draft for review)
**Decision owner (named person):** the managing director
**Window:** before 1 April to 30 June (13 weeks) | after 1 August to 31 October (13 weeks)

## Finding in one sentence

First replies are much faster, but the pilot met the median target and missed the 4-hour target, staff time is unmeasured, and a lower October volume may have helped.

## Decision needed
Roll out to the two other branches, extend the pilot at this branch, or stop. Needed by the next board date, to be confirmed by the managing director.

## Other explanations considered
1. Lower October volume. The export owner says a block of flats moved to another agent on 1 October, so staff had fewer requests to answer. Monthly splits were not run, so the size of this effect is unknown.
2. Seasonality. The before period is spring and early summer; the after period is late summer and autumn. Repair demand differs across the year. A same-season comparison with last year was not available.
3. Complaints are few. 11 versus 4 is too small a count to read as a trend.

## Cost and side effects
One-off cost 1,850 pounds (figure 5). Recurring costs are unmeasured here. The team reports catching two wrong-contractor drafts before sending. Time spent editing is unmeasured, so the net time saving remains open.

## Recommendation (draft)
Extend the pilot at this branch for one more quarter before rolling out, and use that time to close the gaps: count edits, sample 50 drafts for accuracy, take a time sample, and compare against the same season a year earlier. Reason: the median target is met (figures 1 and 2), but the 4-hour target is missed (figure 3) and the accuracy and time measures are open. A wider rollout now would copy the pilot's blind spots to two more branches. This is a recommendation only.
