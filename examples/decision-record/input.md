# Input

All names and figures are invented for this example.

Millbrook Rovers youth club needs one app for fixtures, availability and parent messages. The committee met on 12 March. Rosa Quintero, the treasurer, was named in the minutes as the person who decides purchases under 1,000 pounds a year. She decided on 14 March by email to the secretary: "Go with Fieldday. Review it in September."

Quotes and notes from the committee pack:

- **Option A, shared spreadsheet plus a group chat:** free. The secretary updates it by hand each week. Concern raised by Femi Adeyemi, welfare officer: parents' phone numbers sit in a group chat that any member can export.
- **Option B, Fieldday app:** 480 pounds a year for up to 12 teams. Parents get fixture alerts and reply to availability inside the app. Phone numbers are held by the supplier; the supplier's privacy page says data is stored in the UK. Nobody has read the supplier's data processing terms.
- **Option C, TeamBoard app:** 260 pounds a year for up to 6 teams, 410 for 12. No parent messaging. The club would still need a group chat for messages.

Evidence gathered:
- Last season, the secretary spent about 5 hours a week on fixtures and availability (her own estimate, not logged).
- Seven of nine team managers said in a show of hands they would use an app. Two said they would not.
- The club has 9 teams and expects 11 next season.
- A trial of Fieldday ran with two teams for three weeks in February. 31 of 36 parents responded to availability requests in the app. Before the trial, the typical response in the group chat was not counted.

Committee view: Femi preferred option B only if the data terms are checked. The chair, Lewis Barrett, favoured C to save money. The secretary favoured B.
