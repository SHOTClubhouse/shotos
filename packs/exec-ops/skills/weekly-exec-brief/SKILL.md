# Weekly executive brief

Give a leader one page that says what moved this week, what they must decide and how much of it is proven.

Stage: Track. Rules: 3 Read back the real result, 4 Claim only what you can show.

## Brief format

Use [template.md](template.md) for the brief, including a brief written directly in chat. Keep it to one page and use its section headings.

- Put a separate **Coverage: partial.** line above the decisions whenever a source needed for a material claim or decision is missing, unreadable or incomplete. This applies even when none of the required sources were available. Name the missing records and affected decisions on that line. Use **Coverage: complete.** when all required sources were checked, including sources that were checked and found empty.
- Put each unsupported claim once under **Unverified**, with an inline `[unverified]` label and the evidence needed. Keep the claim itself out of every other section, even as a labelled item, quote or caveat. Other sections may name the missing record or decision and refer readers to Unverified.

## Context and handoff

Start with the last accepted brief and the records for open commitments and decisions. Separate new evidence, unresolved actions and repeated context.

Use `progress-wrap-up` for delivery claims. File one dated brief and link to the owners’ records. For a recurring choice, send the decision question to `decision-brief` and use its Executive Decision Review workflow. In the weekly brief, carry the question, decider, due date and owning record. Carry the decision as agreed only when the authorised person's dated decision is linked from the owning record.

## Start here

**Minimum to start:** the reader, reporting window and the last accepted brief or agreed baseline. If sources are missing, still produce the one-page brief in the template with Coverage: partial and the gaps named. **First output:** the Coverage line and Decisions needed come first, so the reader sees what to decide and how far it is proven. **Handoff:** each decision row names the decider and date, or says what would confirm them.

## If used on its own

With no records, tools or earlier brief, still write the template brief from what the person gives you in chat.

- Put **Coverage: partial.** first and name each source you could not check, such as the task tracker, the last brief or the figures. List every number or status the person asserted under **Unverified** with the evidence needed, and nowhere else.
- With no earlier brief, write "first brief, baseline is what was supplied" and use the stated window.
- Give each decision its decider and date if supplied. Otherwise write "unconfirmed" and what would settle it.
- If `decision-brief` or `progress-wrap-up` is missing, list the question or delivery claim with its owner in the brief. Do not draft options here.
- Mark a commitment as agreed only when the person shows who agreed to it and by when.

## When not to use this

Use a one-line "no material change verified" note when the checked records show no material change and coverage is complete. Use the recipient's reporting format for an investor or board report. Route a decision that needs options to `decision-brief`.

## Steps

1. **Set the window, source cut-off and reader.** Name the reporting period, when sources were checked and the reader's authority. If the reader is not the owner, apply the owner's rule for what can go outward.
2. **Gather evidence for the reporting window.** Compare the last accepted brief with current decision and task records. If this is the first brief, use the agreed reporting window and latest available baseline. Open operating figures, ledger entries, meeting notes, calendars or external sources to confirm a material change, answer a decision question or cover an agreed reporting requirement. Stop gathering once the material changes and open decisions are supported or the remaining gaps are named. Record the sources used and any relevant source that was unavailable, incomplete or empty. If a missing update could change a decision or action, retain the last verified status and date, and name the owner who can close the gap.
3. **Find what changed.** List changes since the last brief, each with a source, date and consequence. For a reported change in a measure, compare period, population, definition and unit. Calculate a change or test significance only for comparable measures. Otherwise show the figures separately and state what comparison is needed. Carry a meeting, proposal or release forward when the evidence shows a changed outcome, a blocker or a decision. A draft, a send, a response and an agreed outcome are separate states.
4. **List what needs deciding.** Give the question, evidenced decision maker, due date and consequence of delay. Mark any missing field as unconfirmed and say what would resolve it. Leave authority unconfirmed if the only named person is the reader, an attendee or a role holder without a decision mandate. Keep detail the reader cannot act on in the linked source record.
5. **Pick external signals worth acting on.** Keep only items that bear on a current decision or commitment. State what each means for the work, and mark it fact, hypothesis or target.
6. **Separate commitments from recommendations.** A **commitment** is something a named person has agreed to by a date, with a source. A **recommendation** is the agent's view. Never let a recommendation or a repeated daily note read as a commitment.
7. **Handle unsupported claims.** A request to include a figure or confirmation is not its source. Keep the gap visible even if asked to omit it.
8. **Preserve the cut-off.** If new evidence arrives after the cut-off and changes a decision, date the update on the same brief and link the owning record. Preserve the earlier evidence window. See the [operating review example](operating-review.md).
9. **Read back the draft before returning it.** Check each factual figure and date against its source. Scan outside Unverified for repeated unsupported claims and remove those copies. Check that the explicit coverage line matches the source gaps.

## Checks

- Every change and figure has a source and date. Comparisons use the same definition, population and period basis.
- Decisions have a sourced decider and due date, or an explicit gap with a next evidence request.
- Commitments and recommendations are in separate sections.
- Nothing repeated from daily notes is reported as a new commitment.
- Unsupported claims appear only under Unverified. The coverage line says partial whenever a required source is missing. "No material change verified" requires that the relevant records were checked.
