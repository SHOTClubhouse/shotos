# Decision brief

Give the decision maker everything needed to choose in about two pages, and show how firm each part is.

Step: Brief. Rules: 2 Agree acceptance first, 4 Claim only what you can show.

## When not to use this

Skip it for a choice that is cheap and easy to reverse, for a question that has no real alternatives, or when the decision has already been made and only needs recording.

## Steps

1. **Write the question.** One sentence, phrased as a choice. "Should we open a second shop in March or wait until autumn?" not "Second shop".
2. **Name the decision maker and the date.** One person decides. State the date by which the choice is needed and what happens if it is late.
3. **Collect facts with sources.** List only what you can show: the figure, where it came from and its date. If a fact comes from one source, say so.
4. **State assumptions.** What the options rely on that is not yet shown. Give each a way to test it and a cheap check, if there is one.
5. **Set out two to four options.** Include "do nothing" or "wait" when it is real. For each: what it involves, cost and effort where known, benefits, risks and what you would have to believe for it to be right.
6. **Recommend one.** Say which option and why, in a few lines. Label it a recommendation. Say how confident you are and what the confidence rests on.
7. **State what would change the decision.** A specific fact or result that would flip the recommendation, and who would see it first.
8. **Note who is affected and who should be consulted.** Flag anything that touches people's data, contracts or money for the right review.
9. **Read back.** Check each fact against its source, and check that no assumption has slipped into the facts list.
10. **For an advisory group.** Add the questions you want the group to answer and hand the brief to the [advisory-session](../advisory-session/README.md) preparation. Record the group's input separately from this brief.

## Output

Use [template.md](template.md). The agent never makes the decision or sends the brief on; the owner does.

## Checks

- The question is a choice with a decision maker and a date.
- Every fact has a source; assumptions are listed apart from facts.
- At least two real options, with trade-offs.
- The recommendation is labelled and says what would change it.
- Nothing is decided, announced or sent.
