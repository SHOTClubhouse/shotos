# decision-brief

**A two-page paper that lets one named person make a significant decision with the facts, options and doubts in front of them.**

The agent writes the question as a choice, lists facts with sources, keeps assumptions apart, sets out options with trade-offs, recommends one and says what would change the recommendation. The decision maker decides. It works alone or as the paper for an advisory group.

It serves the Hypothesise stage. Rules: 2 Agree acceptance first, 4 Claim only what you can show.

## The problem it solves

The owner of Northgate Cycles must decide whether to open a second shop in March. The accountant says the numbers work, the manager says the first shop is stretched, and a supplier says the lease is going fast. Everyone has a view and nobody has said which figures are known and which are hoped for.

With the skill, the paper shows the lease cost and current margin as sourced facts, "demand in the new area matches the first shop" as an untested assumption with a cheap check, three options including waiting, a recommendation to wait and run a four-week pop-up, and the result that would change that.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it before a significant, hard-to-reverse choice. Skip it for cheap, reversible choices, questions with no real alternatives, and decisions already made.

For a recurring leadership choice, use [Executive Decision Review](review-cycle.md) to connect the brief to a recorded decision, handoff and independent next cycle.

## Who it is for

Founders, executives, board members, advisers and anyone who has to put a choice in front of someone else.

## How to use it

> Write a decision brief on whether we open the second shop in March or wait. The decision maker is Dan, needed by 30 November. Use the finance sheet and the lease terms I have attached.

## Needs

Nothing beyond the agent for pasted material. A document connector helps to read finance sheets, contracts or notes you name, read-only.

**Evidence:** Tested in a paired run on 3 November 2026, one run per case, graded by meaning. Cases passed with SHOTos: Haiku 2 of 2, Sonnet 2 of 2, Opus 2 of 2. Without: Haiku 2 of 2, Sonnet 2 of 2, Opus 1 of 2. See [evidence](../../../../docs/evidence.md) for how to read this.

## Tips and common mistakes

- Write the question as a choice with a date.
- Include "wait" as an option when it is real.
- Give the recommendation as one clear view, not a menu.
- Name what would change your mind in advance.

## Limits

- It is only as good as the facts supplied. It will say where evidence is thin.
- It does not give legal, tax or financial advice; it flags where an expert should look.
- It recommends; it never decides.
