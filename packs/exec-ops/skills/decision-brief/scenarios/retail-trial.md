# Should the retailer extend the collection trial?

**Fictional scenario.** The organisation, roles, records and numbers are invented. All outcomes below are simulated.

## Try it

1. Give the assistant [the initial evidence, R1–R3](retail-trial-initial.md). Ask for a one-page decision brief with a recommendation, the approver and the conditions for proceeding or stopping.
2. After you have the brief, give it [the follow-up records, R4–R6](retail-trial-follow-up.md). Ask it to update the decision, handoff and independent-cycle statuses and cite the record for each.
3. Compare both replies with the worked result below. Keep this page out of the assistant's supplied inputs while it works.

## Worked decision brief

**Choice for the retail director by Wednesday 4 March.** Keep the two-store trial, extend to one store with evening collections, or roll out to all stores. Waiting preserves capacity but delays learning whether the result transfers.

**Recommendation.** Extend to one evening site for one week, after its manager confirms capacity. Record the site's own baseline and keep existing staffing. The trial's median improved by 3 minutes with unchanged overtime, but the difference between stores makes the aggregate result insufficient for a wider rollout. Hold if the site cannot supply a baseline or cover the desk. Stop the trial at that site if the manager records an overtime increase attributable to the queue.

## Worked decision and handoff

- **R4.** The director approved one evening site subject to the checks. Wider rollout remains unauthorised.
- **R5.** The regional operations lead accepted the handoff, including access and the first action.
- **R6.** The director later decided to stop at that site. The independent cycle passes because the new operator completed it without intervention and the director accepted the review record.
