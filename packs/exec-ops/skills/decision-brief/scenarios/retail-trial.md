# Should the retailer extend the collection trial?

**Fictional scenario.** The organisation, roles, records and numbers are invented. All outcomes below are simulated.

## Inputs

- **R1, Monday 2 March, trial dashboard.** Two stores tested a new collection-desk queue. The previous four weeks recorded a median wait of 8 minutes across 120 orders. The trial recorded 5 minutes across 118 orders. The measures cover the same weekdays and collection hours.
- **R2, Monday 2 March, store operations check.** Overtime was unchanged. One store accounted for most of the improvement. Neither trial store had the evening staffing pattern of the proposed next site.
- **R3, Tuesday 3 March, decision remit.** The retail director can approve an extension to one store. The regional operations lead runs the review. The receiving store manager controls local staffing.

## Worked decision brief

**Choice for the retail director by Wednesday 4 March.** Keep the two-store trial, extend to one store with evening collections, or roll out to all stores. Waiting preserves capacity but delays learning whether the result transfers.

**Recommendation.** Extend to one evening site for one week, after its manager confirms capacity. Record the site's own baseline and keep existing staffing. The trial's median improved by 3 minutes with unchanged overtime, but the difference between stores makes the aggregate result insufficient for a wider rollout. Hold if the site cannot supply a baseline or cover the desk. Stop the trial at that site if the manager records an overtime increase attributable to the queue.

## Simulated decision and handoff

- **R4, Wednesday 4 March, director's decision note.** Approve one evening site subject to the baseline and staffing checks. No authority for wider rollout.
- **R5, Thursday 5 March, handoff and reply.** The current operator shared R1–R4 and the checklist in the team's work record. The regional operations lead accepted the review scope and dashboard access and agreed to confirm the site and baseline by Friday. The handoff is **Accepted**.
- **R6, Friday 13 March, next review.** The new operator independently found the baseline and fresh results, prepared a brief showing higher evening overtime, recorded the director's dated decision to stop at that site and handed the reset action to the store manager. The observer recorded no intervention and the director accepted the review record. The independent cycle **passes in this simulation** even though the decision was to stop.

## Try it

Give an assistant only R1–R3 first. Ask for the one-page decision brief. Then add R4–R6 and ask which states changed and why. It should recommend a limited test, avoid an all-store claim, and use the actual decision and acceptance records before updating either status.
