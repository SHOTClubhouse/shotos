# Write an explainer for a named audience

Turn a topic into a document that a named reader understands, with examples that work, checked before it is filed or shared.

**Stage:** Operate. **Rules served:** rule 2 (agree acceptance first), rule 3 (read back the real result) and rule 1 (stop at the boundaries).

## When not to use this

Do not use it for an argued opinion piece (use written-content), a press announcement (use press-release), or a quick chat answer. Do not use it when the subject is not understood yet; learn the facts first.

## Steps

1. **Classify it.** Technical: the reader builds or runs the thing. Business: the reader decides or manages with it. External: the reader is outside the organisation (a customer, partner or the public). The class sets the tone, the detail, the sensitive-content rules and who must approve.
2. **Agree the brief.** Name the owner, the reader, what they should be able to do or decide afterwards, the length and where it will be filed or shared.
3. **Gather and source the facts.** List what you will state, each with its source. Mark anything you inferred. Read internal material for what is true, and do not copy private detail into an external explainer.
4. **Draft the mechanism.** Say what it is in one sentence, why the reader cares, then how it works step by step. Define each term the first time it appears.
5. **Add worked examples.** For each key step, show a small example with real-looking inputs and the result, using invented data. Check the arithmetic and the steps by running them where you can.
6. **Illustrate only where a picture shows the mechanism.** A diagram earns its place if it shows parts, flow or sequence that prose would make hard. Do not add decoration. Give every image alt text and label AI-generated images.
7. **Run the fixed QA gate.** Pass or fail each of five: (1) facts: each claim traced to a source and each example re-run; (2) reader fit: right level, right class, nothing the reader should not see; (3) plain English: answer first, short sentences, terms defined; (4) links: all open and point to the right place; (5) accessibility: headings in order, alt text on images, no meaning carried by colour alone, readable contrast.
8. **File it.** Save the final version in the location the owner names, with a short index line (title, class, reader, date, owner, status). Keep drafts and working notes separate from the filed version.
9. **Publish or share only on a yes.** Sending the link, publishing to a site or giving access to others is a separate step. For external explainers, the owner approves the final text first.

## Output

The explainer, the five-part QA gate result, the source list, and a filing note with the index line. A share note is written only after the owner says yes.

## Checks

- The class (technical, business or external) is stated and fits the reader.
- Each key step has a worked example that was checked.
- Every image shows the mechanism and has alt text.
- The five-part QA gate was run and shown.
- The explainer was filed and nothing was shared or published without a yes.
