# Draft a press release from the real relationship

Write an announcement that claims no more than the relationship supports, with every quote approved and every approval on record.

**Stage:** Operate. **Rules served:** rule 1 (stop at the boundaries), rule 4 (claim only what you can show) and rule 2 (agree acceptance first).

## Context and handoff

Read the signed announcement facts, names, dates, quote permissions and embargo. Flag the difference between membership, integration and partnership.

Use `claims-audit` before review. Keep the public copy and approval evidence separately in the announcement record.

Record the approved source brief (file, revision and owner) at the top of the working record or handover, never in the public or buyer-facing copy. If there is no brief yet, the brief step creates it. Every claim traces to the brief or to this skill's own source list; anything else is a question for the owner. If `.work/capabilities.md` exists, read the section for this runtime. If a document tool is missing, say so first, draft in plain text and list approvals still to be obtained and mark the check pending.

## When not to use this

Do not use it to announce something not yet agreed, to invent endorsement, or for a crisis statement that needs legal review. Do not use it to publicise a conversation: a conversation is not news.

## Steps

1. **Classify the relationship.** Pick one from the evidence, not from hope: signed customer (a signed order or contract), pilot (an agreed trial with scope and dates), partner (a signed partnership or joint agreement), letter of intent (a signed non-binding statement), conversation (talks, no signed document). Record the document that proves it. If you cannot find one, the class is conversation.
2. **Apply the headline ceiling.** Signed customer: "X chooses Y". Pilot: "X pilots Y", with scope. Partner: "X and Y partner", only with a signed agreement. Letter of intent: "X and Y sign letter of intent", never "agree a deal". Conversation: no release; offer a holding line or a note to the owner instead.
3. **Agree the brief.** Name the owner, the audience, the outlet and the date, and who in the other organisation must approve.
4. **Write the release.** Headline, a lead that answers who, what, when and why it matters, one body paragraph on what is happening, one on why, background, and a contact. Use plain words. Describe results only as the evidence supports.
5. **Treat quotes as proposed.** Draft quotes in a separate block marked "PROPOSED QUOTE, not approved". Do not place them in the release text as spoken until the named person has approved the exact words in writing. Never put words in a person's mouth for convenience.
6. **Keep evidence and approvals apart.** Make a separate record with the relationship document, each claim with its source, each quote with who approved it and when, and the consent record. The release itself carries none of this.
7. **Run the children check.** If any child under 18 is named, shown, quoted, described by age, school or location, or can be identified: stop. Default is to remove them. If they stay, written consent from a parent or guardian is needed for the exact wording and image, the organisation's safeguarding lead signs off, and the consent covers this use and outlet. Use first names only if the guardian agrees and nothing else identifies the child.
8. **Run the claim check.** Every number, date and statement traces to evidence. Labels such as "leading" or "first" need proof.
9. **Hand over.** Give the owner the release, the proposed quotes, the evidence and approvals record, and the children check. Releasing to press, posting or emailing is the owner's decision and waits for a yes.

## Output

Three parts, kept apart: the release draft, a block of proposed quotes marked not approved, and an evidence and approvals record. Use [template.md](template.md) for the record.

## Checks

- The relationship class is stated with the document that proves it.
- The headline does not claim more than the class allows.
- No quote appears as said before written approval.
- The release contains no evidence log or consent detail.
- Anything involving children has a consent record and a safeguarding sign-off, or has been removed.
- Nothing was sent to press or published.
- Reviewer check: pick the headline and confirm the relationship document in the record supports its wording; confirm no quote is unmarked.
