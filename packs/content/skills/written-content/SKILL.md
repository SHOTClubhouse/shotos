# Write a paper, report, one-pager or blog post

Produce a written piece that a named reader can follow, that is true line by line, and that does not go out until the owner says so.

**Stage:** Operate. **Rules served:** rule 2 (agree acceptance first), rule 4 (claim only what you can show) and rule 1 (stop at the boundaries).

## Context and handoff

Read the publication brief, reader question, approved voice sample and strongest primary sources. Choose the format from the job the piece must do.

Use `plain-english-check` for a review pass if needed. Keep the draft and evidence with the publication record.

Name the approved source brief (file, revision and owner) in the first line of the output and cite it for every claim. Anything not in the brief is a question for the owner. If `.work/capabilities.md` exists, read the section for this runtime. If a link or document tool is missing, say so first, write plain text and list links as unchecked and mark the check pending.

## When not to use this

Do not use it for emails, replies or short messages, for legal or regulated text, or for a piece whose argument the owner has not decided. Use `explainer` when the task is to explain how something works to a named audience.

## Steps

1. **Agree the brief.** Name the owner, the type (paper, report, one-pager or blog post), the reader, what the reader should do or believe afterwards, the length and the channel. Write the check that will show it worked.
2. **Choose the type.** A paper argues one idea with evidence. A report records findings and what changed. A one-pager gives a busy reader the point and the next step. A blog post makes one point in a human voice. Say which and why.
3. **Gather sources first.** List every fact you may need with its source: a document, a dataset, a public page or the owner's own records. A fact with no source is not used, or it is marked "owner to confirm".
4. **Build the six-part arc.** Situation: what is true now. Problem: what is wrong or at risk. Evidence: what shows it. Idea: what you propose. How it works: the mechanism, step by step. What to do next: one clear action for this reader. A one-pager compresses each part to a few lines; it does not drop any. Show the six names (Situation, Problem, Evidence, Idea, How it works, What to do next) as headings or line labels so the owner can see each part.
5. **Write the draft.** One idea per paragraph, answer first, plain words, sentences with verbs. Give each number or claim a source tag in the working copy, and keep the tags in a source table.
6. **Check people and consent.** Name no real person, customer or child without written consent. Use "a letting agent" or an invented example, and label it illustrative. Quotes need the speaker's approval.
7. **Label what is what.** Mark each figure as sourced, estimated or illustrative. Never present an illustrative number as fact.
8. **Run the fixed QA gate.** Pass or fail each item and fix before going on: (1) every fact has a source in the table; (2) the six parts are present in order; (3) the reader and the next step are clear in the first paragraph; (4) no unconsented names; (5) no unsourced or invented figures; (6) plain English and spelling as the owner chose; (7) links open and point where they say; (8) images have alt text and rights.
9. **Hand over for review.** Give the owner the draft, the source table and the gate result. Say what is unverified.
10. **Publish only on a yes.** Publishing, posting, emailing or uploading to a live site is a separate step. Prepare it, show what will happen and where, and wait for the owner's yes.

## Output

The draft, a source table (fact, source, status), the QA gate result with pass or fail per item, and a list of unverified items. Use [template.md](template.md) for the brief and arc. A publish note is written only after the owner says yes.

## Checks

- Every fact in the draft appears in the source table.
- All six parts of the arc are present, in order.
- No real person is named without recorded consent.
- The QA gate was run and the result is shown.
- Nothing was published, posted or sent.
- Reviewer check: pick any three figures in the draft and find each in the source table with its status.
