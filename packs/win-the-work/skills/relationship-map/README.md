# relationship-map

**Map who is connected to whom in a market or account, label how sure you are of each link, and pick one first test.**

The agent reads your notes and builds four tracks (customers, partners, investors, advisers). Each row runs from person to organisation to opportunity to evidence to owner to next action. Every link is labelled confirmed, reported, proposed, historic or unresolved. It ends with a list of gaps and one cheap first test.

It belongs to **Scope**. It serves rule 4, Claim only what you can show, and rule 1, Stop at the boundaries.

## The problem it solves

Millbrook Rovers youth club wants local sponsors and a grant. The chairman says "we know the owner of the garage, and his cousin is on the council". Three weeks later a volunteer emails the garage owner, who has never heard of the club's request, and the "cousin" turns out to be a former councillor. The club had reported links, treated them as confirmed, and spent its one introduction on a guess.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it when starting on a market, inheriting an account, or choosing who to approach first.

Do not use it for one contact with nothing around them, or to build files on private individuals. Keep to professional roles and links that bear on the work.

## Who it is for

- Founders and chairs working a local or sector network.
- Account owners taking over a relationship.
- Fundraisers who need to know which introductions are real.

## How to use it

> Here are my notes on the sponsors and funders around the club. Build the relationship map, label every link, and tell me the one thing to test first.

You get the tables from [template.md](template.md). Correct any label you know to be wrong; that is the point.

## Needs

Nothing beyond the agent. A mail export or CRM extract adds evidence; the skill only reads it.

## Tips

- Date every link. A historic link is useful only if it says when.
- Keep a person on two tracks if they belong on both; the asks are different.
- Run the first test before building a longer map.

## Limits

- The map is as good as the notes. It does not research people online unless you ask and the sources are named.
- Labels are judgements. The skill states its reasons; you decide.
