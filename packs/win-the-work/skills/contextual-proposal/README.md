# contextual-proposal

**Research the reader, write a proposal argued for them, and prove every claim, asset and page before calling it ready.**

The agent researches who will read the proposal and what they decide, writes the argument for that reader, keeps a claim ledger and an asset register, explains the money in the reader's terms, and renders and inspects every page or slide. It stops at "ready for review"; the owner sends.

It belongs to **Operate**. It serves rule 3, Read back the real result, rule 4, Claim only what you can show, and rule 1, Stop at the boundaries.

## The problem it solves

Northgate Cycles sends the same proposal to a council depot, a school trust and a hotel group. The depot's buyer measures cost per bike-week; the trust cares about safeguarding and parent trust; the hotel cares about guest experience. The proposal quotes "over 500 repairs a year", which nobody can source, and includes a photo of a customer's child on a bike. Two slides overflow in the exported file because nobody opened it.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it for a named reader and a real decision.

Do not use it for a generic one-pager, or for many near-identical proposals (see [one-off-or-factory](../one-off-or-factory/README.md)).

## Who it is for

- Founders and sales leads writing bids and pitches.
- Fundraisers writing to a funder.
- Anyone who has sent a proposal with a wrong name or a broken page.

## How to use it

> Write a proposal for the school trust's facilities manager. Research what the trust says it cares about first, keep a claim ledger and an asset register, and render every page before you tell me it is ready.

You get the proposal and a working sheet from [template.md](template.md). Read the ledger first: anything marked unsupported has been removed or flagged.

## Needs

Nothing beyond the agent for writing. Opening or rendering the file needs a way to produce and view it (a document tool, a presentation tool, or an export to images). Public web research needs your approval of sources.

**Evidence:** Tested in a paired run on 3 November 2026, one run per case, graded by meaning. Cases passed with SHOTos: Haiku 2 of 2, Sonnet 2 of 2, Opus 2 of 2. Without: Haiku 2 of 2, Sonnet 2 of 2, Opus 2 of 2. See [evidence](../../../../docs/evidence.md) for how to read this.

## Tips

- Ask for the argument sentence before the draft; if it is weak, the proposal will be.
- Treat "unknown rights" as "do not use".
- Look at the last page. Mistakes cluster there.

## Limits

- The agent can only check claims against the sources it is given.
- A rendered check by the same agent that wrote the file is a discipline, not an independent review. Ask a person to look at anything high stakes.
- Rights notes record what you tell it; they are not legal advice.
