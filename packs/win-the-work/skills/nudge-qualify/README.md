# nudge-qualify

**Score an opportunity on Numbers, Understand, Definition, Gatekeepers and Elevate, and get a go, pause or walk-away call with the next question to ask.**

The agent reads your notes on an opportunity, scores five questions from 0 to 3 with the evidence beside each, marks what is unknown as unknown, and recommends go, pause or walk away. It ends with one question, to a named person, that would improve the weakest score.

It is used at **Scope** and again before committing to a proposal, mainly during Explore and Agree in LEAP. It serves rule 4, Claim only what you can show, and rule 1, Stop at the boundaries.

## The problem it solves

Northgate Cycles, a small bicycle retailer, is asked to build a fleet-maintenance service for a council depot. The meeting went well, the buyer was friendly and the founder is already drafting a price. Nobody has asked who approves the spend, what the depot pays now, or whether the repairs can be done at the volume mentioned. The founder spends two weeks on a proposal that a procurement committee was never going to read.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it after a first or second meeting, before a proposal, and again before signing.

Do not use it on signed work, or on a request so small it costs less to do than to score.

## Who it is for

- Founders deciding where to spend proposal time.
- Sales leads who want a consistent bar across deals.
- Account owners presenting a case to a manager or board.

## How to use it

> Here are my notes from two meetings with the council depot. Score the opportunity with NUDGE and tell me whether to write the proposal.

You get the scorecard from [template.md](template.md). Challenge any score; the evidence column shows where it came from.

Read the pursuit recommendation and pricing gate separately. Go may mean one procurement conversation. Draft a priced offer only when the gate is cleared for drafting.

## Needs

Nothing beyond the agent.

## Tips

- Write what the buyer said, not what you hope they meant.
- A zero is useful. It tells you what to ask.
- Rerun the scorecard after each meeting; scores should move because evidence arrived.

## Limits

- Scores are a structured judgement, not a measurement. The thresholds are defaults you can change.
- The skill cannot discover facts that are not in your notes.
- A friendly buyer can still be a weak opportunity; the card exists to show that.
