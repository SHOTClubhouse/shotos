# NUDGE: qualification quick reference

**Use NUDGE to decide whether the next hour belongs on an opportunity.** Score the opportunity from notes or a call, not the person. A commercial owner decides whether to pursue; the agent supplies the evidence and gaps.

| Question | Ask |
|---|---|
| **N**umbers | What do they gain and spend? What do we gain and spend? Is there mutual benefit and a funding route? |
| **U**nderstand | What is the challenge in their words, and what happens if it remains? |
| **D**efinition | What work is being asked for, by when, and can we deliver it? |
| **G**atekeepers | Who can say yes or no, and what has each actually said or done? |
| **E**levate | What is the alternative, including doing nothing, and what checkable difference do we offer? |

Score each question **0 unknown, 1 weak or hearsay, 2 reasonable evidence, 3 strong and confirmed**. Put the source and status beside every score. The total is a prompt for judgement, not a probability or an automatic decision.

**Go** means pursue a bounded next step when there is a real problem, a credible buyer or route in, a plausible funding route and a timely action. **Pause** means there may be a fit but no useful action until a named blocker or event clears. **Walk away** means there is no viable mutual benefit or route to a deal, the work cannot be delivered, or the contact is gathering competitive information. A low score on alternatives, an unknown exact budget amount or an unconfirmed signer does not alone stop pursuit.

**Separate pursuit from commitment.** A go may mean a procurement call. It does not authorise a priced proposal or a promise. Resolve weak Numbers, Definition or Gatekeepers evidence before pricing or committing. Name one next question to the person who can reduce the biggest decision risk. Send nothing without the owner's approval.
