# Decision record: one-off or factory

**Work:** 12 one-page renewal letters to landlords
**Owner (accepts):** the managing director
**Date:** 2 November
**Items planned:** 12, due end of next week
**How much items differ:** moderately (single flat, portfolio, two special-rate cases)

## Acceptance for one item
- Correct landlord, property and new fee.
- The reason for the change is stated in plain words.
- Special-rate landlords are treated as the managing director expects.
- Fits one page; the managing director would sign it without editing.

## Routes compared
| | Direct authoring | Factory |
|---|---|---|
| Build time | none | about 8 hours (guessed by the lead) |
| Time per item, including checking | about 25 minutes (guessed) | unknown; checking still needed |
| Expected rework rate | low, unknown | unknown |
| Total for 12 items | about 5 hours (12 x 25 minutes, guessed) | about 8 hours plus checking and rework |

## Pilot of three
Not run. Recommended items: one single-flat landlord, one portfolio landlord, one special-rate landlord. Write each directly, time it, and have the managing director accept or reject. A factory pilot is not needed unless the direct figures turn out far worse than 25 minutes.

## Decision
**Author directly.**
**Because:** at 12 items, the factory's build time alone (about 8 hours, guessed) exceeds the whole direct route (about 5 hours, guessed). The factory would have to produce better accepted quality to justify the extra time, and nothing suggests it would. The guessed figures could be wrong in either direction, so the first three direct letters double as the pilot: if they average over 45 minutes each, revisit.

## What would change this
- The count rises to 40 or more.
- The direct pilot shows each letter takes much longer than 25 minutes.
- The managing director asks for the same letter every quarter, which would amortise the build.
