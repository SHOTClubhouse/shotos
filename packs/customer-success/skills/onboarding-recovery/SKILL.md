# Onboarding recovery

Work out where an onboarding stopped, pick the smallest next step that gets the customer to first value, and prepare the message for a person to send.

Stage: Operate. Rules: 1 Stop at the boundaries, 3 Read back the real result, 4 Claim only what you can show.

## When not to use this

Skip it when the customer has asked to cancel or has a complaint that needs the owner first (escalate to the owner). Skip it for an account that has not started and has no stall. Use [success-handoff](../success-handoff/README.md) if the problem is who owns the account.

## Steps

1. **Define first value.** Ask or find what first value means for this customer: the first outcome they would call a success (for example, "first weekly report received", "first booking made"). If it is not written down, propose one and ask the owner to confirm.
2. **Gather the evidence.** Read the thread, calendar (meetings held, missed, booked), onboarding plan, call notes and any usage record. Build a short timeline with dates and sources.
3. **Diagnose where it stopped.** Find the last confirmed step and the first missed one. Name the likely cause only as a hypothesis, one of: waiting on the customer, waiting on us, unclear ownership, blocked by a dependency, lost interest, or unknown. State what evidence would confirm it.
4. **Choose the smallest next step to first value.** One step the customer can complete in under 30 minutes, if possible. Prefer removing a blocker for them over adding a meeting. If the stall is on our side, the next step is ours.
5. **Assign owner and date.** One person for our step, one for the customer's step, each with a date. If the customer's owner is unknown, write "to confirm" and make that the first question.
6. **State what the customer must do** in plain words, in order, with how long it takes.
7. **Draft the message** from the real thread, in the sender's voice. Short, one ask, one date, an easy way to reply. Do not blame, do not imply a contract term the evidence does not show. Save as a draft; never send.
8. **Set a check.** A date when someone will look again, and what counts as recovered (the first value event happened, not "they replied").
9. **Read back.** Check every date and name in the message against the thread. Report anything unverified.

## Output

Use [template.md](template.md): timeline, diagnosis with confidence, next step, owners and dates, customer asks, draft message and recovery check.

## Checks

- First value is defined, and recovery is measured against it.
- The diagnosis is labelled a hypothesis unless the evidence is direct.
- There is one next step, with an owner and a date on each side.
- The draft quotes the real thread and was not sent.
- A stall caused by us is stated as ours.
