# success-handoff

**Hand an account to the person who will deliver for it, with stage, health and transfer status kept separate and a source for every fact.**

The agent starts with the account record and fills specific gaps from mail and notes. It labels each fact as stated, observed or inferred and tracks Prepared, Offered, Accepted and Completed separately from lifecycle and health. The receiving lead confirms the offered revision in writing.

It serves the Own stage. Acceptance is the receiver’s written agreement. Completion requires the customer notification, owner update and first action. Rules: 1 Stop at the boundaries, 4 Claim only what you can show.

Practise with the [conditional acceptance exercise](conditional-acceptance.md) to check who owns the work while access and the ownership update are pending.

## The problem it solves

Harbourside Lettings signs a property management contract with a landlord group. The founder emails a summary to the new customer success hire and tells the landlord "Asif will look after you". Asif has not read it, the "health: good" label came from a friendly call and the promised weekly report was never written down. Three weeks later the landlord asks for the report and nobody owns it.

With the skill, health says "unknown, one friendly call" and cites it, the weekly report appears under promises with its source, transfer status reads Offered until Asif signs the acceptance record, and the introduction message is a draft for the founder to send.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it when an account moves between people, from sales to delivery, or back to a founder. Skip it for a short note or an account staying with the same owner.

## Who it is for

Customer success leads, account managers, founders and delivery leads.

## Needs

Mail and document connectors, read-only, to build the brief; a document connector with write access to the folder you name for the record. Calendar and transcript connectors are optional. Without them, paste the material and the brief says what it could not check.

## Tips and common mistakes

- Never write "healthy" without evidence; "unknown" is a useful answer.
- Put every promise in writing with a date, even the casual ones.
- Do not announce the handoff to the customer before the receiver accepts.
- Keep the acceptance record with the account.

## Limits

- The brief is only as complete as the records. Promises made verbally are missing unless the giver adds them.
- Health ratings are judgements; the skill shows the evidence but you decide.
- The agent cannot mark acceptance. Only the receiver can.
