# Audit the claims before the document goes out

Step: Check. Rules: 4 (claim only what you can show) and control 10 (claim discipline).

A reader trusts a document until one claim fails. Find the weak claims first, and let the owner decide.

## When not to use this

Skip internal notes and private drafts. This is not a legal or regulatory review and it does not judge style.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the document and its owner.** Name the document, the audience and the person who accepts changes. If there is no owner, ask.
2. **Read it all.** Extract every factual claim: numbers, dates, comparisons ("faster than"), superlatives ("leading"), named customers, awards, quotes, guarantees, legal or compliance statements and statements about what a product does. Give each a number.
3. **Find the source for each claim.** Use only evidence provided or visible: a file, a report, a system figure, a link. Record where it came from and its date. Do not invent sources and do not search for support and then call the claim supported on a weak match.
4. **Set a status.** Use exactly one:
   - **supported:** a source exists, is dated, and says this.
   - **outdated:** a source exists but is old, or the number has moved; give its date.
   - **unsupported:** no source was found.
   - **illustrative:** an example or a made-up figure; it must be labelled as one in the document.
5. **Apply the number rule.** Any figure about users, customers, revenue, growth or results needs a dated source and the definition used (for example "registered accounts" against "active this month"). If the definition is missing, mark it outdated or unsupported.
6. **Propose a fix.** For each claim that is not supported: remove it, soften it to what the source shows, add the date and definition, label it illustrative, or get the evidence. Give the replacement wording.
7. **Rank the blockers.** List the claims that should stop the document being sent, such as unsupported figures, named customers without permission, and legal or safety statements.
8. **Present and stop.** Give the table, the blockers, and a question for each decision. Make no edit to the document before the owner's yes. If the request already asks you to fix the document, that is the yes for the fixes: make exactly those fixes, show the difference, and report every claim you could not fix as unsupported. If the owner says to keep an unsupported claim, keep it but list it as unsupported and chosen by the owner, and do not mark it supported.

## Output

Use [template.md](template.md): a numbered claims table and a blockers list.

## Checks

- Every factual claim in the document is in the table.
- Every status is one of the four words, and every supported claim has a dated source.
- Every number about users, customers or revenue has a date and a definition, or is not marked supported.
- The document was not changed before the owner's yes (a request to fix it counts as that yes).
