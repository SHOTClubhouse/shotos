# Check sensitive data before work continues

Step: Brief. Rules: 1 (stop at the boundaries) and 4 (claim only what you can show).

Harm to people, especially children, cannot be undone by a later fix. Ask the questions first.

## When not to use this

Skip work that holds no personal data. This is not legal advice. A data protection officer or a designated safeguarding lead decides the hard cases.

## Steps

1. **Describe the work and the data.** List each item of data the work collects, stores, shares or publishes, who it is about, and why. Mark which items are special-category (health, ethnicity, religion, sexuality, biometrics, criminal records, and similar) and which concern children.
2. **Check minimisation.** For each item ask: what decision does it support? If none, recommend removing it. Prefer less detail (a yes or no) over more (a diagnosis).
3. **Check the lawful basis and the consent record.** For each purpose, ask what basis is relied on and where it is recorded. Ask to see the record, not a statement. If consent is the basis, it must be specific, recorded, and easy to withdraw.
4. **Check parental consent for children.** For anyone under the age that applies in your country, ask who gave consent, when, for what, and where it is stored. Check that it covers each use, including photos and any sharing.
5. **Check retention.** Ask how long each item is kept, why, who deletes it and when.
6. **Check access.** Ask who can see each item now, and who should. Compare with sharing settings, exports and backups. Recommend named roles over groups.
7. **Check images of children.** Ask about permission for taking, storing and publishing each image, whether names are attached, whether a child could be located from it, and who can withdraw it.
8. **Check the safeguarding path.** Ask who is the named person for concerns, how a worker raises one, and what happens in the first 24 hours. If none is named, that is a finding.
9. **Mark and decide.** Mark each check met, not met or unknown, with the evidence seen. Give a decision: proceed, proceed after fixes, or stop and escalate.
10. **Stop when consent is missing.** If consent or its record is missing for any item that needs it, do not build, move, publish or send anything. Name the person to escalate to, say what they need to decide, and wait.

## Output

Use [template.md](template.md): a checklist with evidence, and a decision line.

## Checks

- Every data item is listed with its purpose, and unneeded items are flagged.
- Each of the checks above has a status and the evidence seen, or "unknown".
- Missing consent produced a stop, not a workaround.
- No personal record was copied into the report.
- A named person is given for escalation.
