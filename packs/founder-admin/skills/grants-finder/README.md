# grants-finder

**Find grants, screen them for eligibility, then evidence, then budget, and draft applications without submitting any.**

The agent profiles your organisation, finds candidate grants from sources you approve, removes any you are ineligible for, checks what evidence each wants, weighs the budget and effort, and gives you a ranked shortlist with deadlines and fit. For any grant you pick, it drafts the application from approved material.

It belongs to **Scope**. It serves rule 4, Claim only what you can show, and rule 1, Stop at the boundaries.

## The problem it solves

Millbrook Rovers youth club needs 6,000 pounds for new goalposts and a storage unit. A volunteer finds seven grants, spends a weekend on an application, and learns in the last paragraph of the guidance that the fund only supports registered charities, which the club is not. The deadline for the next best option passes while they are rewriting.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it to find funding, or to decide which grants are worth the hours.

Do not use it for loans, investment or sponsorship, or to send the same text to many funders.

If you already have an invitation or a named fund, check that round's terms and use the [application pack](application-pack.md). It keeps the answers, approved budget and private evidence in the same work record. The [fictional application exercise](application-example.md) shows what changes when the applicant supplies an official deadline and confirms its cash contribution.

## Who it is for

- Club chairs, charity trustees and founders looking for funding.
- Operations leads who write applications.
- Anyone asked to "find some grants".

## How to use it

> We are an unincorporated youth football club in a small town, 120 members, no paid staff. We want 6,000 pounds for goalposts and storage. Find and screen grants, tell me what is eligible and what we would need, and draft the best one. Do not submit anything.

You get the screen from [template.md](template.md). Check the eligibility lines first; they decide everything else.

## Needs

Nothing beyond the agent. Public web access to funder pages helps; you approve the sources.

**Evidence:** The 3 November version ran once per case, graded by meaning. Both conditions passed both cases on Haiku, Sonnet and Opus. The current application pack has a separate fictional budget and approval exercise. See [evidence](../../../../docs/evidence.md) for the evaluation history.

## Tips

- Give the agent your governing document and last accounts. Eligibility turns on them.
- Always check deadlines against the funder's own page before acting.
- A "no" in the eligibility screen saves hours. Welcome it.

## Limits

- Funder pages change. Every deadline carries the date it was read.
- The agent cannot judge a funder's unwritten preferences.
- It does not submit, register on portals or contact funders.
