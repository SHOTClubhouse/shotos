# Investor update

Tell investors what actually happened, including what went badly, in numbers they can trace, and ask them for something specific.

Stage: Track. Rules served: 4 (claim only what you can show), 3 (read back the real result) and 1 (stop at the boundaries).

## Context and handoff

Read the last update, approved metrics, current period and commitments. Reconcile denominators and dates before reporting movement.

Use `decision-brief` for a board ask. Store the approved update and linked metric sources with investor records.

## When not to use this

Skip it for a fundraising pitch (that is persuasion; this is reporting), for legal or regulatory notices, and for an announcement of material news that needs advice before disclosure. If an investor asks for non-public information that others have not received, stop and ask the owner.

## Steps

1. **Set the period and the reader.** Month or quarter, dates covered, who receives it and what they have told you they care about. Note anything investors were promised in the last update.
2. **Collect approved material.** Ask for the sources: management accounts, bank balance, sales or usage reports, the last update, the board minutes, and a list of what the owner has approved for sharing. Use only these. Anything else is "not approved" and goes in the held-back list, not the draft.
3. **Build the numbers table.** For each figure: name, value, period, source, date of the source and status (confirmed from a record, owner's statement, estimate or forecast). Typical: cash at bank, monthly spend, runway in months (state the formula and the assumption), revenue, key customers or users, headcount, one or two leading indicators. Never round in a way that flatters. Compare with the previous period and with what was promised.
4. **Write the update:**
   - **Headline** in two sentences: how the period went.
   - **Numbers** table.
   - **What went well** with evidence.
   - **What did not** with the cause and what is being done. Do not omit a miss because it is uncomfortable.
   - **Next period:** goals with dates.
   - **Asks:** specific requests (an introduction to a named kind of person, a view on a decision, a hire); each says what a yes helps with.
5. **Run the traceability check.** Every number, name and date appears in the table with a source. Forecasts are labelled forecasts. Customer names appear only with permission. No personal data about individuals.
6. **List what was held back** and why, so the owner can decide.
7. **Stop.** Do not send, schedule or post. The owner reads, edits and sends.

## Output

Use [template.md](template.md): the update, the numbers table with sources and the held-back list. Save with the project, for example `.work/investor-updates/2026-q3.md`.

## Checks

- Every figure has a source, a date and a status.
- Bad news is included, with cause and response.
- Forecasts and estimates are labelled.
- The asks are specific and honest.
- Nothing unapproved appears; held-back items are listed.
- The status is draft and nothing was sent.
