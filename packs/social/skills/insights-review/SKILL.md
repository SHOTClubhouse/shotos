# Review the month and adjust the mix

Stage: Track, feeding Sustain and Scale. Rules served: 3 Read back the real result, 4 Claim only what you can show.

## Context and handoff

Read the reporting period, metric definitions and prior baseline. Reconcile differences in denominators before calling a change.

Use `content-calendar` to turn the strongest finding into a test. File the evidence with the channel review.

## When not to use this

- No export or screenshots are available. Ask for them; do not recall or estimate numbers.
- The period has fewer than about ten posts. Say the sample is too small to change the mix and keep it.
- The person wants proof that a single post caused a sale. Insights show association, not cause.

## Steps

1. **Fix the frame.** Ask for: account, platforms, the month, the plan (the calendar or the mix target), the audience (consumer, business or both), the one goal metric that matters most (for example sign-ups, or business enquiries), and the owner who decides. Ask for the platform export (CSV or dated screenshots) and note for each file its date range and the date it was exported.
2. **Record the sources.** List every file used with date range and export date. Every number in the review will cite one. If the platform defines a metric differently from another platform (reach, views, impressions), say so rather than adding them together.
3. **Compare plan and actual.** Count what was planned and what went out, by pillar (education, community, promotion). Note slots that were skipped and why (from the owner).
4. **Compare results by pillar.** For each pillar report: posts, total and average of the metrics the owner cares about (for example reach, saves, shares, comments, link clicks, follows), and the best and worst post with its ID and date. Use medians where one post dominates. Label every figure with its source file. Keep these apart and never add them together: reach (people) and impressions or views (counts); followers and non-followers; organic and paid or boosted; platform results and link clicks recorded in the owner's own analytics. For a business audience, weight enquiries, clicks to a partnership or pricing page and replies from named organisations above likes. For a consumer audience, weight saves, shares, joins and bookings. Vanity counts (likes, follower totals) are reported but do not drive the mix.
5. **Say what the data does not show.** Small samples, one viral post, a holiday, a paid boost, a change of posting time, missing months. Do not claim a cause.
6. **Look at the audience's side.** Say which audience each result reflects; a post that did well with parents says nothing about buyers. Note the questions and comments that recurred (from `engagement-review`), and any complaints or safeguarding flags raised, without personal details.
7. **Propose next month's mix.** Start from the current mix. Make a small change, usually no more than ten percentage points, with a reason tied to the numbers, and a way to tell whether it worked ("if saves on education posts rise after two weeks, keep it"). If the evidence is thin, keep the mix and say what to collect.
8. **Hand to the owner.** The review is a draft recommendation; changing the calendar or boosting anything needs the owner's yes. Use [template.md](template.md). Ask for a decision and record the new mix and the date. The owner may overrule the proposal.

## Output

A filled [template.md](template.md): sources, plan against actual, results by pillar, limits, proposed mix with a test, and the owner's decision.

## Checks

- Every number cites a file, a date range and an export date.
- No number is estimated or recalled from memory.
- Claims of cause are absent or labelled as a guess.
- The proposed change is small, reasoned and testable.
- The owner decides; the new mix is recorded only after their yes.
- Reach and impressions, organic and paid, follower and non-follower figures are not blended, and each result is tied to the audience it reflects.
- The goal metric is named, and vanity counts do not drive the proposed change.
