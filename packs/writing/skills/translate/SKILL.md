# Translate so the meaning survives

Produce a translation that says what the original says, in the voice the reader expects, with names, numbers and terms intact.

**Stage:** Operate. **Rules served:** rule 2 (agree acceptance first), rule 3 (read back the real result) and rule 4 (claim only what you can show).

## Context and handoff

Read the source, locale, glossary and any names or numbers that must survive unchanged. Flag ambiguity before translating commitments.

Use `plain-english-check` for a separate readability review. Keep source and translated version linked with reviewer notes.

Cite the approved source text revision; the translation covers only that text. If `.work/capabilities.md` exists, read the section for this runtime. If a file or transcript tool is missing, say so first, translate pasted text and list files as unread and mark the check pending.

## When not to use this

Do not use it as the only check for contracts, medical or safety instructions, safeguarding material or anything with legal effect: a qualified human translator signs those off. Do not translate private messages or personal data without a reason the owner can state.

## Steps

1. **Agree the job.** Source and target language (and variety, such as Brazilian or European Portuguese), the reader, the purpose (to understand, to publish, to subtitle), the register (formal, friendly), any length limit (captions, posts), and who accepts it.
2. **Build the glossary first.** List names, brand and product terms, job titles, units, prices and dates, and decide for each: keep as is, translate as, or ask. Reuse the owner's existing glossary if there is one.
3. **Prepare transcripts.** For speech, work from the transcript. Keep speaker labels and timestamps. Mark words the transcript was unsure of as unclear; never guess them into the translation.
4. **Translate.** Translate meaning, not word for word. Keep the structure the reader needs (headings, captions within length, one subtitle per timestamp).
5. **Check what must not change.** Compare source and target for every number, date, time, price, name, link and glossary term. List every mismatch and fix it.
6. **Back-translate key passages.** Translate the most important sentences (the ask, prices, instructions, anything a reader will act on) back into the source language and compare meaning. Note any drift and fix it.
7. **Flag for a fluent reviewer.** Mark anything public, legal, medical, safety-related or involving children for review by a fluent person before use, and say so in the handover.
8. **Hand over.** Present the translation, the glossary, the mismatch list (empty when it passes), the back-translation notes and the review flags. Do not publish, send or post without the owner's yes.

## Output

The translated text in the same structure as the source, the glossary, a numbers-and-names check, back-translation notes for key passages, and a list of passages needing human review.

## Checks

- Every number, date, price, name and link matches the source.
- Glossary terms are used consistently.
- Key passages were back-translated and the meaning holds.
- Unclear transcript words are marked, not guessed.
- Public, legal, medical, safety and children's material is flagged for a fluent reviewer.
- Nothing was published or sent without the owner's yes.
- Reviewer check: pick one price or date and confirm it matches the source; read the back-translation of the main ask.
