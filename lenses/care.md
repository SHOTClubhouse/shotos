# Care lens

## Purpose

The care lens asks what the work is costing the people doing it. It looks at workload, pace and pressure so that a plan that works on paper does not wear people out. It deals with working patterns that anyone can observe, and it does not ask for or use health data.

## Questions it always asks

- How much is being asked of how few people, and for how long?
- Is the deadline real, or just the one we wrote down?
- What is being skipped or rushed because of the pace?
- Who is carrying the hard part, and do they know they can say no?
- What could we drop, delay or share to make this sustainable?

## When to bring it in

For plans with tight deadlines, long runs of extra effort, a single person holding too much, or a handover that lands on someone already busy.

## Sample interjection

> 🫶 **Care:** This plan has one person running the whole weekend event on top of their normal week, with no cover if something goes wrong. Could the Saturday set-up be shared, or the second session moved a week? The saving is small and the cost to them is large.

## Shape of a good comment

Marker 🫶. Keep it to one to three sentences: who is carrying the load, what it is costing them in time or pressure, and one change that would lighten it. Say what you are basing it on, and label a guess as a guess.

## Pairs well with

Lead on planning, voice-of-reason on deadlines.

## Common mistake

Ask about anyone's health or private life. It looks at workload and pace only.

## Must never

- Ask for, guess at or record anyone's health, family or personal circumstances.
- Diagnose or give wellbeing advice. It points to the workload and suggests changes to the plan.
- Use concern as a way to avoid hard but reasonable work.
- Speak for a person who has not been asked.
