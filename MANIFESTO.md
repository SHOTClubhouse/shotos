# The SHOTos manifesto

The way good teams build things has not really changed. They find the real problem, agree what good looks like, ship small pieces into real use, measure what happened and leave the work in capable hands. What has changed is who does most of the typing. Agents now draft, code, research and send. That makes the old disciplines matter more, because a team can now produce far more output with none of the proof.

SHOTos names that way of working as a five-step work loop: Brief, Build, Check, Hand over and Improve. It is set out in [docs/framework.md](docs/framework.md). The principles below are what we hold to inside every step.

## What we hold to

**1. Claim only what you can show.**
Installed is not executed, and executed is not accepted. A report says what was checked, what could not be checked and what is a guess. An honest gap is fine. A confident claim with no evidence is not.

**2. People decide; agents do the work.**
Every piece of work has a named owner. Agents act within clear limits and stop before anything external, costly, destructive or public unless someone with authority says yes. Being able to do something is not permission to do it.

**3. Count accepted work, not generated work.**
An agent can produce a hundred drafts in an hour. We count what a named person accepted and what changed because of it.

**4. Agree what good looks like before building.**
We describe success as checks a person or a test can verify, including at least one case that should fail. Where output varies, as AI output does, we keep a set of examples and check against them every time.

**5. Earn autonomy, one task at a time.**
We prove a way of working on a handful of real items before running it on hundreds. Trust grows from a record of accepted work, not because a tool allows it.

**6. Every output has a human owner before it ships.**
If nobody will run it, maintain it or answer for it, it does not ship, however good it looks. Help from outside should leave a team more capable, not more dependent.

**7. As much process as the risk needs, and no more.**
A one-line fix needs one check. A change that touches customers, money, safety or children needs independent review. Process that protects nothing is waste.

## Where these ideas come from

None of this stands alone, and we would rather say so. SHOTos draws on continuous delivery, the DORA research into software delivery, product thinking over project thinking, Team Topologies, specification by example and behaviour-driven development, lean practice, and current work on evaluating AI systems and keeping people in control of them. What SHOTos adds is the small set of controls that agent work needs, written so non-engineers can use them too. Sources are in [docs/sources.md](docs/sources.md).
