Run a partner pilot

Use this workflow when two organisations have agreed to try something and the next step depends on several people. Start with the accepted deal or written agreement. Keep it as the source for commitments, and use the programme record to link decisions, evidence and ownership across the pilot.

WhenWho owns the next decisionWhat to record
Define the pilotThe commercial owner confirms the agreed scope with the partner.Accepted revision, scope, baseline and exclusions. Use Contextual Proposal if terms are still being drafted.
Agree how to judge itThe pilot owner and sponsor agree the measure and stop conditions.An acceptance card with a threshold, comparison period, evidence source and review date.
Decide whether to startThe pilot owner confirms staffing and access. The authorised owner approves the live activity and the communications owner reviews any public copy separately.Named leads, permissions, dependencies and the launch decision. Use Pilot Before Scale and Claims Audit.
Review the resultThe sponsor decides whether to proceed to Own, change the pilot, hold or stop.An evidence pack and a dated decision record.
Transfer ownershipThe receiving lead confirms access and accepts the handoff. They then run a cycle independently before the sponsor considers expansion.The agreed first action, handoff receipt and separate independent-cycle evidence.

Try it with a fictional partner

Start with the North Quay worked deal. The club accepted proposal P2 and Robin accepted the delivery handoff. The baseline is two of four registers complete by Monday. The club still controls access to its register.

Use the following fictional updates in order. After each one, update the programme record with the decision, the evidence and the next owner.

  1. 9 November. Morgan has yet to name the register lead. The parties agree to judge the four-session pilot against three of four registers completed by Monday. The sponsor asks whether the team can start.
  2. 13 November. Morgan confirms the lead, setup and permitted access. The authorised sponsor approves the pilot. The partner also asks for a public announcement, but the communications owner has yet to approve copy.
  3. 9 December. The club records three of four registers complete by Monday. The sponsor accepts the result for the pilot group and authorises an independent cycle with the receiving lead.

Use the partner pilot workflow and programme record. Cite the accepted agreement and the new evidence for each decision. Show the current stage, who owns the next action, and which permissions are still pending. Keep simulated results labelled as fictional.

Compare your decisions
  1. Hold the start. Robin needs to confirm the lead and permitted access before setup. The accepted deal and delivery handoff are still evidenced.
  2. Start the pilot. Record the sponsor's approval for the agreed scope. Keep the announcement pending with the communications owner.
  3. Proceed to Own. Record the result against the baseline and threshold. Keep expansion open until the receiving lead's independent cycle is evidenced.

If the pilot changes scope or the partner asks for a wider rollout, record the new decision and its owner in the same programme record. Keep the accepted scope and each decision available to the receiving team.
