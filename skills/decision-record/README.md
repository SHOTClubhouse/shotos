# decision-record

**Write one page that says what was decided, by whom, on what evidence, and what would change it.**

The agent drafts a short record: context, options with their costs and risks, the decision, the named decider, evidence labelled measured, inferred or opinion, a trigger that would reopen it and a review date. It records decisions made by an authorised person or body and names the person responsible for following up.

It works at any stage, most often Track. It serves rule 4, Claim only what you can show, and rule 1, Stop at the boundaries, because a record is not an approval.

## The problem it solves

At a fictional youth organisation, Millbrook Rovers youth club, the committee chose a new fixtures app in March. In September a new volunteer asks why the cheaper option was not picked. The treasurer remembers a price; the secretary remembers a data concern; nobody remembers who agreed. The club spends an evening re-arguing it.

A one-page record would have shown the three options, the safeguarding concern about stored phone numbers, the treasurer as decider, and the review date, which was last month.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it for decisions others will rely on, including supplier choices, designs, policy and scale-pivot-stop calls. Skip it for trivial, easily reversed choices.

## Who it is for

- **Business unit leader or sponsor:** a record that survives staff change.
- **Engineering or practice lead:** the same shape as an architecture decision record.
- **Product owner:** reasons for what was left out.
- **Solo founder:** your future self will not remember why.

## How to use it

Ask:

> Write a decision record for the fixtures app choice. Rosa Quintero, the treasurer, decided on 14 March to go with option B. Here are the quotes and the committee notes.

Check the options and evidence against the controlling record. Use the [committee decision example](committee-example.md) to practise separating the decision, action approval and delivery.

## Needs

Nothing beyond the agent.

**Evidence:** Tested in a paired run on 3 November 2026, one run per case, graded by meaning. Cases passed with SHOTos: Haiku 2 of 2, Sonnet 2 of 2, Opus 2 of 2. Without: Haiku 2 of 2, Sonnet 2 of 2, Opus 2 of 2. See [evidence](../../docs/evidence.md) for how to read this.

## Tips and common mistakes

- Record the decider's name, not "the committee".
- Keep dissenting views. They are the most useful part later.
- Write the change trigger as something you can observe.
- Never edit an old record to match what happened; write a new one that supersedes it.

## Limits

- A record can be wrong or one-sided. It shows reasoning, not correctness.
- It does not give anyone the authority to act on the decision.
