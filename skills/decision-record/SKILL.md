# Write a decision record

Capture a decision while the reasons are fresh, in one page, so that later readers can see what was chosen, why, and what would reopen it.

Stage: any, most often Track. Rules: 4 Claim only what you can show, 1 Stop at the boundaries.

## Context and handoff

Read the original decision, options, constraints and who had authority. Record the decision date and what would reopen it.

Use `progress-wrap-up` when communicating impact. Keep the decision in the existing project log and link to the evidence.

## When not to use this

Skip it for a trivial or easily reversed choice that no one will ask about. Do not use it to make the decision: the agent records a decision the authorised person or body has made or drafts options for them to choose between.

## Steps

1. **Name the decider.** Record the person with authority, or the formally authorised body if the decision belongs to it. For a body, cite its mandate and name the person accountable for following up. Advisory support and an individual vote do not establish a decision. If authority is unclear, keep it open.
2. **State the context.** Two to five sentences: the situation, the constraint, the deadline and what happens if nothing is decided. Link to the problem statement or evidence pack rather than repeating it.
3. **List the options.** At least two real options, plus "do nothing" where it is a real choice. For each give what it costs, what it gains, the main risk and how reversible it is. Include the option that was rejected first and why.
4. **Record the decision.** One sentence in plain words, with the controlling record, date and scope. Include conditions and exclusions that affect what can happen next. If the decider has not decided yet, write "Draft, awaiting the decider" and give a recommendation labelled as such.
5. **Attach the evidence.** List the facts that carried the decision, each with a source and date. Mark which are measured, which are inferred and which are opinion. Keep opinions that disagreed with the outcome and say who held them.
6. **Say what would change it.** Name at least one observable trigger: a number crossing a line, a cost rise, a supplier change, a failed test. This is the contrary case for the decision.
7. **Set a review date.** Pick a date or an event after which someone looks again. Say who reviews.
8. **Note what this supersedes or depends on.** Link earlier records. Mark the older one "superseded by" with the new one's number.
9. **Check authority.** Name the next action, its owner and what proves completion. Carry exact authorisation from the controlling record and list any further approval or condition separately. A recommendation, discussion note or drafted record cannot confer authority. Use the [committee decision example](committee-example.md) to check the difference.

## Output

Use [template.md](template.md). Save in the project's decisions folder, numbered, for example `decisions/0007-choose-booking-supplier.md`. Never edit an accepted record to change the outcome; write a new one that supersedes it.

## Checks

- The decider is a named authorised person or body. For a body, the record cites its authority and names the person following up.
- At least two options with a downside for the chosen one.
- Evidence items have sources and are labelled measured, inferred or opinion.
- A change trigger and a review date are present.
- The recorded decision, action approval and execution status each match their own evidence.
