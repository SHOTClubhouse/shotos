# Check which source applies to the decision

A fictional operations lead wants to know whether a supplier's standard plan offers weekend support for a November rollout.

| ID | Source |
| --- | --- |
| S1 | A July supplier announcement says “weekend support is now available”. The announcement describes a summer pilot, with no list of eligible plans. |
| S2 | The supplier's schedule effective 1 November lists weekday support for Standard. It lists weekend support under Extended, subject to local coverage. |
| S3 | A November trade roundup repeats the July announcement. Its link points to S1. |
| S4 | The supplier's account lead says Extended “should be available locally”. No site confirmation or price quote is supplied. |

> Brief the rollout lead using these four records. Say what Standard includes, whether the team can plan on weekend cover, and what would change the decision.

## What to check

- Standard lists weekday support under the schedule that applies in November. Cite S2.
- S1 describes the summer pilot. S3 repeats S1, so its later publication adds no independent confirmation.
- S4 supports reporting what the account lead said. Local weekend coverage and price remain unverified.
- Recommend planning around weekday support or holding the weekend option pending a site-specific confirmation and quote. Keep the recommendation separate from the verified terms.
