# retrospective

**Look back at the work without blame, find the causes in the system, and leave with a few changes that each have an owner and a date.**

The agent prepares the page: what we expected (from the records), what happened (a timeline), the gaps in both directions, why they happened, and up to five changes with one owner, a date and a measure each. People supply their accounts. The write-up avoids blaming anyone and passes candidate rules to the learning loop.

It supports Track and Sustain. It serves rule 3, Read back the real result, and rule 4, Claim only what you can show.

## The problem it solves

A team at a fictional firm, Harbourside Lettings, finished a pilot two weeks late. In the meeting, the blame went to a developer who had changed the contractor list on a Friday. Everyone left unhappy. Nothing changed, and the next pilot also slipped.

The retrospective looks at the timeline: the contractor list had no owner, there was no review before a Friday change and nobody had agreed what "ready" meant. It leaves three changes, each with an owner and a date, and records that the Friday change was a symptom of a missing review step.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it at the end of a pilot, release, incident or phase, and after a surprise in either direction. Skip it for tiny tasks. Do not use it to judge individuals.

## Who it is for

- **Small team lead:** a short routine that produces changes, not complaints.
- **Product owner:** compares the plan with what happened.
- **Engineering or practice lead:** a blameless review for incidents and releases.
- **Enablement coach:** shows teams how to ask "what in our setup made this likely?"

## How to use it

Ask:

> Prepare a blameless retrospective on the repair-reply pilot. Use the acceptance card, the evidence pack and the release log. I will run the meeting and fill in the causes with the team.

Share the page with the people involved. Change the draft where they disagree.

## Needs

Nothing beyond the agent. The meeting is run by a person.

**Evidence:** Tested in a paired run on 3 November 2026, one run per case, graded by meaning. Cases passed with SHOTos: Haiku 2 of 2, Sonnet 1 of 2, Opus 2 of 2. Without: Haiku 1 of 2, Sonnet 2 of 2, Opus 2 of 2. See [evidence](../../docs/evidence.md) for how to read this.

## Tips and common mistakes

- Start with the record of what was expected, not memories.
- Include what went better than expected.
- "Be more careful" is not a cause. Ask what made the slip likely.
- Five changes at most, each with one owner.
- Follow up on the dates, on the same page.

## Limits

- An agent cannot see what people felt or left unsaid. Hold the conversation with people.
- It will not fix a team that cannot speak freely. That needs the sponsor.
