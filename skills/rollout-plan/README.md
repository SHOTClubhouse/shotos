# rollout-plan

**Take what worked to the next team by copying the method, and make each new team measure its own starting point.**

The agent checks the Track evidence, the accepted independent cycle from Own and the sponsor's rollout decision. It identifies what each team can reuse, what each team must check locally and where to stop. The sponsor approves the proposed sequence and support.

It supports Sustain and Scale. It serves rule 1, Stop at the boundaries, rule 3, Read back the real result, and rule 4, Claim only what you can show. It is the practical form of control 9, pilot-before-scale.

## The problem it solves

At a fictional firm, Harbourside Lettings, the repair-reply pilot worked at one branch. The managing director says "roll it out to the other two". Branch two has different request types and a messier helpdesk export. Branch three has no repair log at all. If the first branch's 5.5 hours is quoted as the expected result everywhere, branch three will look like a failure and branch two a success for reasons that have nothing to do with the method.

The plan starts with branch two, takes its own baseline first, holds branch three until it has a log, and puts the improvement slot every four weeks.

## When to use it, and when not to

Use it after a scale decision, and before copying a process, skill set or agent setup to another team, site or product. Skip it when no one has decided to scale, or the first team still needs its builders.

## Who it is for

- **Sponsor or business unit leader:** a staged plan that keeps your control.
- **Enablement coach:** who moves, for how long, and what the new team can teach itself.
- **Engineering or practice lead:** a plan that reuses skills and evals.
- **Operations leader:** governance that allows local changes without losing the standard.

## How to use it

Ask:

> The repair-reply pilot at the Harbourside branch is approved to scale. Draft a rollout plan for the other two branches. Branch two has a helpdesk export; branch three has no repair log. Start no more than two teams. I approve.

Correct the sequence and people, then approve or change it.

## Needs

Nothing beyond the agent. The plan sends nothing to the teams.

**Evidence:** Tested in a paired run on 3 November 2026, one run per case, graded by meaning. Cases passed with SHOTos: Haiku 2 of 2, Sonnet 2 of 2, Opus 2 of 2. Without: Haiku 1 of 2, Sonnet 0 of 2, Opus 0 of 2. See [evidence](../../docs/evidence.md) for how to read this.

## Tips and common mistakes

- Carry the method. Never carry the first team's figures.
- Start small. Check, then widen.
- Name who moves and until when, or the first team becomes a permanent help desk.
- Keep the boundary list the same everywhere unless the sponsor changes it.

## Limits

- Readiness is judged from what the agent can see; a conversation with each team still has to happen.
- A plan does not create the time or the people.
